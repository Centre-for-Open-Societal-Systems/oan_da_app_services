DA Platform Architecture

How the Development Agent portal reaches the DA Services backend, how DA Services depends on the DA Registry, and which external systems it integrates with. Compiled from the FSD (D3), the Part 1 HLD, the team's architecture diagram (3 Oct) and the staging UI.

1. System context

The UI never talks to a database or to another system directly. Every request goes through Kong to DA Services. DA Services owns the operational data (visits, tasks, leave, KPIs) and fetches the DA's identity and posting from the DA Registry. Farmer, grievance and survey data stay in the systems that own them; DA Services keeps only references.

2. Who owns which data

The rule from the HLD: a fact lives in exactly one authoritative system; everyone else keeps a reference. DA Services therefore stores what it creates and fetches what it only displays.

3. Three requests, end to end

Where the data comes from is easiest to see on real pages.

3.1 DA opens the dashboard

sequenceDiagram
  participant UI as DA Web Portal
  participant K as Kong
  participant S as DA Services (Frappe)
  participant R as DA Registry (OpenG2P)
  participant F as Farmer Registry
  UI->>K: GET /api/v1/dashboard/summary (JWT)
  K->>S: forward (token valid)
  S->>S: resolve user → role, scope, da_id (RBAC)
  S->>R: GET DA by da_id (name, photo, assigned Kebeles)
  S->>S: count visits, tasks, sync queue from own DB
  S->>F: farmer names for today's visits
  S-->>UI: one response: header, tiles, today's plan

About 90% of the dashboard is DA Services' own data. The Registry contributes only the DA's name and posting.

3.2 Supervisor approves a leave request

sequenceDiagram
  participant DA as DA (mobile)
  participant S as DA Services
  participant SV as Supervisor (portal)
  participant R as DA Registry
  participant M as MoA registry
  DA->>S: POST /api/v1/leave/requests
  S->>S: validate balance, save request (own DB)
  SV->>S: POST /api/v1/leave/requests/{id}/approve
  S->>S: decrement balance, write audit, notify DA (SMS + app)
  S->>R: push lifecycle outcome: active_status = On-leave (idempotent)
  R-->>S: receipt
  R->>M: sync status change (Registry's job)

Leave is DA Services' record. The Registry only learns the resulting status, because the Active Status column on the master registry is defined by the FSD as "set by Part 2 lifecycle".

3.3 DA raises a grievance for a farmer

sequenceDiagram
  participant DA as DA (mobile, maybe offline)
  participant S as DA Services
  participant G as Grievance Service
  DA->>S: POST /api/v1/grievances (category, subject, farmer_id, photo)
  alt online
    S->>G: create case (Grievance Service API)
    G-->>S: external_case_id, status
    S->>S: store link {da_id, farmer_id, external_case_id, status}
  else offline / API down
    S->>S: queue operation (Awaiting sync), retry later
  end
  S-->>DA: ticket id or "queued offline"
  G-->>S: status updates (polled or webhook) → SMS to submitter

Triage, response, escalation and closure happen in the Grievance Service. DA Services shows them read-only and keeps only the link and the offline queue.

4. How a DA gets into the system

Onboarding is the Registry's workflow (Yash). DA Services only picks up the result: an approved, active DA becomes a user with a role, a scope and a DA-ID.

flowchart TD
  A[CSV import / Webintake / MoA sync] --> B[Auto checks: Fayda match, duplicate, mandatory fields]
  B -->|pass| C[DA-ID issued, record in shadow state]
  B -->|fail| X[Exception queue: resolve manually]
  C --> D{Supervisor review}
  D -->|Approve| E[Publish to Registry Gen 2]
  D -->|Reject| R[Closed]
  D -->|Request changes| A
  E --> F[Publish to MoA, separate step with retry]
  F --> G[DA Active, SMS sent]
  G --> H[DA Services: user + RBAC assignment with da_id and Woreda scope]

Machines do the checks; a person makes the decision. Approval and publication are separate states, and MoA publication has its own retry and reconciliation.

5. Roles and scope

Authorisation is deny-by-default and enforced in the backend on every call. A role says which actions exist; the RBAC assignment says over which Region, Woreda or DA.

RoleScopeCanCannot
Development AgentOwn DA-IDOwn profile, farmers, visits, leave requests, feedback, grievancesApprove anything, see other DAs
SupervisorOwn WoredaApprove onboarding and lifecycle, assign Kebeles, approve leave, team viewsAct outside own Woreda, manage users
ExecutiveOwn RegionRegional dashboards, SLA roll-ups, reportsAny edit, approval or assignment
OAN AdministratorAllUsers, roles, scopes, settings, sync config, auditDelete immutable audit records
Communications OfficerBroadcast domainBroadcasts, alerts, knowledge articlesSee or edit DA identity, approvals

  • Auth integration, RBAC and scope (DA-27)

  • Leave, lifecycle, KPI, reviews, training, advisors, surveys
  • Adapters: Registry, Farmer Registry, Grievance, ODK, messaging

7. Open decisions

  • Registry integration path. Does DA Services call OpenG2P directly or a Registry API layer? Determines the registry client, status push-back and whether events or polling are used.
  • Login. oan_auth_service as interim (same as Grievance) vs central OAN IAM. Does Kong validate the token or only route?
  • Fayda sign-in. Needs relying-party credentials, a sandbox and HTTPS on staging. Planned as a second sign-in once available.
  • Registry pages in the portal (/agents, /approvals, /assignments, /registry-sync). Served through DA Services as pass-through, or by OpenG2P's own UI?
  • Field list. Fayda ID, mandatory fields, dropdown values, Zone level, joining date; naming (Name / Father / Grandfather vs First / Last) and birth date (EC month-year vs full Gregorian) differ between Shikha's list and the UI.
  • Supervisors per Woreda and whether any one of them may approve, or a primary approver exists.
  • Master data source for Region / Zone / Woreda / Kebele.
